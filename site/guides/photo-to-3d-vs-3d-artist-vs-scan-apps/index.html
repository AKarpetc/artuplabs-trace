Photo to 3D vs a 3D artist vs scan apps

Three ways to get 3D models of furniture for a Shopify store. We compare time, cost, accuracy and what each needs from you — using our measured numbers for photo → 3D and saying “varies” where we have no source.

Our numbers come from our own runs in October 2026: 10 furniture photos and several live runs through the app's pipeline; the cost is the generator's list price per run, not a retail price. Where we have no source for another option, the table says “varies” instead of guessing.

Photo → 3D

The only option that starts from what a store already has. It is fast enough to run over a catalog, and you can reject a bad result and try another photo. The trade-off is accuracy: the back is inferred and the size has to be set. Details: what works and what fails.

A 3D artist

The most accurate option and the only one that handles configurable products well (fabric swatches, finishes, sizes). It is also the slowest and most expensive per product, which matters when the catalog has hundreds of items. Shopify's help suggests finding a 3D modeler through its Partner Directory and asking for a GLB. (source: Shopify Help: hire a 3D expert) Give the artist Shopify's checklist (about 4 MB, textures up to 2048 px) so the file loads fast. (source: Shopify 3D model standards checklist)

Scan apps (Polycam, KIRI Engine)

Phone apps that build a model from many photos (photogrammetry) or a LiDAR scan. Polycam and KIRI Engine both have free plans. (source: Polycam pricing) (source: KIRI Engine pricing) They need the physical item and a careful capture around it, and they struggle with the same materials as photo → 3D: glossy, transparent and very thin parts. Check which export formats your plan includes before you scan a whole showroom.

How to choose

  • Hundreds of products, only photos: photo → 3D for the catalog; an artist for the few hero products.
  • Items in a showroom, simple materials: a scan app is worth trying.
  • Configurable or high-ticket products: a 3D artist.
